# Focused research and opportunity assessment — 2026-10-11

Question: does query-conditioned resource selection add useful source quality beyond cheaper fixed selection when resources are heterogeneous?

Source records:

- ReSLLM, Wang et al., https://arxiv.org/html/2401.17645v1 . Methods3.1–3.3, evaluation4.2–4.4 and representations5.4 inspected. Select/rank uncooperative resources from names/URLs/descriptions; pointwise yes/no judgments; logged snippet judgments can be aggregated for training. FedWeb supplies real historical resources and external relevance judgments. Our pilot preserves metadata-conditioned selection and independently judged resource quality; substitutes pinned typed Jev for the paper's LLM logits and does not reproduce SLAT training. Model/representation effectiveness varies; richer snippets after tuning are not automatically better.
- Resource Selection for Federated Search on the Web, Nguyen et al., https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.04556 and PDF inspected. Examines historical real web search engines and cautions about sparse descriptions and skewed collection sizes. Corpus relevance and source-size estimates matter. Our experiment avoids assuming two nested title/fulltext indexes represent heterogeneous resources.
- Can LLM Rerankers Predict Their Own Ranking Performance?, Ni et al., https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03535 . Abstract screening only. Self-consistency/calibrated performance estimation better motivates a later reranking-benefit study than raw score margins. Deferred until routing study completes; no new confidence/model-grade claims or experiment from this abstract.
- NIST FedWeb2014 official task, https://trec.nist.gov/data/federated2014.html . Metadata/query list/RS README/qrels inspected for structure; only two exposed grade excerpts7015/7044 were read during source discovery and are assigned to development. Official nP@5 uses linear UDM-weighted resource precision normalized by the best possible five resources; qrel scores0–1000 are not ordinal relevance grades to exponentiate. Missing judgments follow official zero convention and are reported separately.

New evidence justifying follow-up: the original EXP-112 model produced17 fulltext selections and7 empty selections falling back to fulltext. Therefore its apparent quality gain may be a fixed-policy result, not evidence that query adaptation helped. Preserve an explicitly retrospective fixed-control audit; do not relabel the original inconclusive outcome or call the audit preregistered.

Exclusions: static BEIR reruns cannot establish heterogeneous-source selection; original FedWeb pages/snippets are unnecessary for this resource-quality screen and are not acquired; no provider fine-tuning; wrapper planning/stopping/cache issues444–448 remain untouched. Full methods for the reranking-confidence paper await a separate opportunity. Original #516 cohorts and stop rules remain untouched.
